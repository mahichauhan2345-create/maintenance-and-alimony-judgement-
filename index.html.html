Doctrinal Evolution & Key Thematic Trends (2021–2026)

Synthesis of major jurisprudential shifts in Indian maintenance and alimony law.

1. Start Date & Retroactive Protection

Reaffirming the roadmap in Rajnesh v. Neha, rulings like Shahjahan (2025) established that maintenance must be granted from the date of filing the application. Courts actively reject attempts by husbands or lower tribunals to delay payments or restrict maintenance solely to the date of the order, shielding wives from systemic judicial backlogs.

2. Qualification vs. Earning Capacity

In cases like Anamika Jain (2026), the Supreme Court clarified that a wife's high professional qualification (such as an MBA or technical degree) or abstract capacity to earn does not automatically disqualify her. Unless she is actually gainfully employed with sufficient income to maintain her marital standard of living, her maintenance entitlement remains intact.

3. Bigamous & Void Marriages Protection

The landmark 3-judge bench ruling in Sukhdev Singh v. Sukhbir Kaur (2025) settled conflicting views by holding that spouses of void or bigamous marriages under Section 11 HMA are fully entitled to claim interim maintenance (Sec. 24) and permanent alimony (Sec. 25), while giving courts equitable discretion based on conduct.

4. Dynamic Alimony & Periodic Escalation

To counter inflation and eliminate repetitive modification litigation, modern judgments like Rakhi Sadhukhan (2025) introduced structured financial mechanisms—such as mandatory biennial percentage escalations (e.g., 5% every two years)—ensuring long-term economic security for dependent spouses.
