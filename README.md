# maintenance-and-alimony-judgement-
Team - Mahi Chauhan, Section B and Vidhi Kapoor, Section B
Topic - Our topic is maintenance and alimony for wives and children in matrimonial disputes decided by the Supreme Court of India between 1 September 2021 and 31 August 2026. Maintenance is an ongoing and very common everyday issue: almost every family that breaks down has to decide who pays for whom. It interests us because the Court's rulings decide how much protection a wife or child actually gets.
How we found the judgments - We searched SCC Online and Indian Kanoon using terms such as "wife entitled to maintenance", "alimony", "section 125 CrPC" and "maintenance". Together these gave roughly 60 to 70 Supreme Court cases. We excluded roughly 40 to 50 of them and kept 20. We kept a judgment if the Court gave reasons, if it was decided inside the date window, and if the main question was maintenance or alimony for a wife or child arising from a matrimonial dispute. We excluded cases decided before the window, a motor accident compensation case, and cases where maintenance was only mentioned in passing or was not decided. Some divorce judgments under Article 142 are included because the Court also fixed alimony in them. We started with a narrower topic, a wife's entitlement under s. 125 CrPC, and widened it after screening because the narrower topic gave too few judgments. The source link for each judgment in the data sheet is the Court's own copy.
What the data shows - Judgments per year where Seven of our 20 judgments were decided in 2024, eight in 2025 and two in 2026 (up to August), against two in 2023 and one in 2022. We were surprised that we found none from 2021 and only one from 2022. The appellant or petitioner got the main relief in 14 judgments and partial relief in 5. Only one appeal was dismissed, which surprised us. Nine judgments were criminal appeals, nine were civil appeals and two were other petitions. Most of the criminal appeals are s. 125 CrPC cases, while the civil appeals are mostly Hindu Marriage Act and Article 142 matters, which did not surprise us. What did surprise us was how many divorce cases the Court decided with alimony as part of the order. The page index.html shows the same data by provision and lists every judgment with its holding.
Limits - These 20 judgments are a sample found through our searches, not every Supreme Court maintenance judgment in the period. Twenty cases are too few to show a firm trend, because adding one or two cases would change the counts per year. We did not record amounts of maintenance as numbers, so we cannot say how much courts usually award.
AI-use disclosure - We used two AI tools. Claude (Anthropic) helped us understand long judgments, decide whether each one fitted our topic, and draft some one-line issue and holding entries. It also made the three charts, a draft of the webpage and a draft of this README from our final data sheet, and checked our spreadsheet for mistakes in dates and dropdown values. Gemini (Google) was used to create the HTML for the webpage. We did not treat what either tool said about a judgment as the source. We read each full judgment from the Court's own copy, compared the issue and holding in the data sheet against it, and cross-checked each other's rows. We corrected the entries where they did not match, and we reviewed the charts, the page and this README before publishing.
